# Agent 4 — evaluation and statistical validity

Researcher: native Codex subagent; no DeepSeek execution. Date: 2026-11-01. Scope: common forecast comparisons, proper scoring, temporal integrity and statistical claims. No data processing, training or execution was performed. Full proposed protocol and direct primary citations: [evaluation methodology](../07_evaluation_methodology.md).

Established literature: proper scoring rules support distribution comparisons; multivariate scores differ in dependence sensitivity; block bootstrap requires assumptions about temporal dependence/stability; multiplicity correction is separate from search/test contamination. Sources: [Gneiting and Raftery](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437), [Scheuerer and Hamill](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/22327/), [Politis and Romano](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870), [Holm](https://www.ime.usp.br/~abe/lista/pdf4R8xPVzCnX.pdf).

Deduction: marginal scores cannot establish an advantage from the joint coupling itself. Test a marginal-preserving shuffled-path competitor and residual copula. Hypothesis: learned paths improve barrier-event probability forecasts after controlling for marginal quality; null: no improvement. Compare recursive, direct-independent, joint Gaussian and flow on common origins, equal sample counts and fixed features. Requires complete multi-step targets and enough independent time blocks. Cost increases with path sample count and vector dimension; use chunked scoring and paired samples where appropriate.

Proposed hierarchy: empirical random-walk, EWMA Gaussian/t, seasonal and statistical baselines, regularized/tree baselines, small neural probabilistic baseline, structured Gaussian, then flow. At minimum beat the strongest validation-selected conventional baseline on CRPS with calibrated intervals. A deterministic forecast's poor density score is not evidence of learned distribution skill.

Proposed threshold: 2% practical improvement plus adjusted inferential support, adequate calibration and stable signs across windows. This is a project decision, not a universal scientific rule. Failure means retaining the simpler model or declaring insufficient evidence. Competing inference approaches include block bootstrap and HAC loss-difference tests; both become unreliable with too few effective periods or strong unmodeled breaks. Preserve the entire item cross-section when resampling time blocks.

Most consequential assumption: an untouched final test exists. The lead's reconnaissance reports prior retrospective development. Recommendation: prospective final test after protocol freeze, with retrospective analyses explicitly exploratory. Other limitations: unknown latency and revisions, sparse outcomes, missing execution, survivor bias, cross-item dependence and many search choices. High confidence in the protocol; predictive and economic feasibility unverified.
